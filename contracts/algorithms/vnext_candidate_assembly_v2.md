# vNext candidate assembly and inventory, version 2

Status: implementation contract under development for slice 18.5. These algorithms are not registered runtime capabilities. The frozen `vnext-contracts-1` suite and existing job descriptors remain unchanged. A consuming suite must pin the completed algorithms, schemas and conformance fixtures before public proposal activation. D8 remains a separate prerequisite for registration and promotion.

This supplement resolves byte assembly, section/unit identity transfer, inventory, explicit correspondence and named-unit authorization details within the candidate leaf's existing authority boundary. An assembled text, identity map or inventory is not a deterministic preservation pass, registered candidate, semantic verdict or current verified authority. Integrated preservation reports, registration and qualification remain implementation work.

## Byte assembly

`patch-assembler-v2` operates on an admitted canonical patch and immutable base coordinates. It revalidates patch identities and bindings, payload hashes, target spans, operation kinds, dependencies, declared operation/section budgets and payload shapes before assembly. It never infers an editing decision from payload text. The caller must independently validate admission and operator-response provenance.

Body replacement covers only the target's direct body. Subtree replacement/deletion covers the target and all descendants. Before/after insertion and child append use the exact base subtree boundary. Replacement/deletion of a document root is prohibited. Base intervals must not overlap; repeated mutation targets are rejected symmetrically unless both operations are insertions. A body replacement plus an insertion at that same target cannot become valid merely by changing operation labels.

Apply insertions sharing a physical byte boundary in canonical topological order. Distinct address spellings, such as after one sibling and before the next, do not create different ordering domains. Each later insertion at a boundary must directly depend on the previous insertion there. The base-coordinate sweep emits insertions at a boundary before a replacement beginning at that boundary; it must not reverse after-anchor insertion order.

Retain exact source-piece ranges before normalization and the base/payload origin of every assembled heading. Reparse the combined text. It must contain exactly the expected headings at their expected pre-normalization offsets; missing newlines and fence spillover cannot silently consume or create a heading. Retained base headings must retain their actual parent. New subtree roots must land under the declared parent: a later append cannot attach a child to a newly inserted sibling. These combined checks supplement local payload grammar.

`whetstone-markdown-normalization-v1` converts CRLF and CR to LF, removes trailing spaces/tabs from each line, removes empty terminal lines, then emits exactly one final LF. Apply it once to the combined text, before any candidate hash or validation verdict. Reparse normalized bytes and require unchanged heading text/level/parent topology. JSON artifact canonicalization remains the separate `whetstone-canonical-json-v1` algorithm. No promotion-time normalization or version stamping is allowed. Any future system version operation must be explicit and applied before this normalization/hash boundary.

## Section identity transfer

`vnext-section-lineage-v2` produces `section-identity-map-v2`. Its extra `identity_algorithm_version` field identifies this algorithm. Its active rows form exact physical ATX preorder, numbered from one. Append retired rows in their previous map order, continuing the ordinal sequence. Active parents must be earlier active rows with a shallower heading level. Retired rows retain historical hashes, headings and parent IDs; an active parent's later level must not reinterpret a tombstone's historical level. Every parent reference must still resolve to an earlier row. No active ID may also be retired.

An unchanged base heading identified by its exact assembly origin retains its stable ID. Its `previous_stable_section_id` equals that same ID; `created_by_operation_id` continues to identify its original creation, if any. A replacement subtree whose operation explicitly preserves identity retains its target root ID even when that root is renamed. A replacement that does not preserve identity creates a new root with `previous_stable_section_id` naming the replaced root and retires the old identity.

For descendants under an identity-preserving replacement, use deterministic structural correspondence:

1. A complete unchanged ordered child stream preserves every corresponding child, including repeated headings. Its structural signature records heading text, level relative to the subtree root and normalized direct-body hash.
2. Otherwise match unique complete subtree signatures among unmatched siblings, then unique sibling heading names, then unique subtree signatures with the root title omitted. These rules support distinct reordered siblings, a rewritten body under a unique heading, and an unambiguous rename. Recursively inspect matched children.
3. An exact unmatched subtree may move across parents when only one predecessor and successor share its structural signature. Recurse inside the moved subtree so unchanged repeated descendants retain their identities. A consistent absolute heading-depth change does not mint new identities.
4. Multiple unresolved same-name sibling matches or multiple unresolved identical moved-subtree matches are ambiguous and fail. Never pick an arbitrary ordinal, similarity score or model-supplied identity.

Structural correspondence establishes section identity only. It does not prove that a rewritten requirement retained meaning or authorize the removal of its units. Every base identity not transferred becomes or remains a retired tombstone. A new identity must not equal any active or retired ID in the base map.

The first inserted heading in an operation's complete payload preorder retains the frozen D6 root identity formula: `sec_` plus SHA-256 of canonical JSON `['whetstone-inserted-section-id-v1', transaction_id, operation_id]`. For every further newly inserted payload heading, use `sec_` plus SHA-256 of canonical JSON `['whetstone-inserted-section-descendant-id-v2', transaction_id, operation_id, payload_preorder_ordinal]`. The ordinal counts all payload headings, including ones that retain predecessor identities; it is not renumbered after transfer. This provides unique descendant identities without changing the frozen root formula. Root and descendant domains share the section collision role.

Map validation checks exact current heading spans/hashes and row topology. Registration must also independently reproduce assembly and the complete map from the immutable base, canonical patch and payloads; a structurally plausible standalone map is insufficient provenance.

## Seed preservation inventory

`preservation-inventory-v2` separates primary byte coverage from overlapping protected lexical facts. Primary units use the qualified exact-line scanner's `heading`, `body`, `separator`, `fence_delimiter` and `fence_body` kinds. They partition every UTF-8 source byte, including preamble, line endings and absent final newlines. Each unit records source offsets, exact and normalization-equivalent content hashes, owning stable section ID, normative classification/source, protected status and features.

Preamble and heading-free content use a non-addressable inventory owner: `sec_` plus SHA-256 of canonical JSON `['whetstone-frontmatter-section-id-v2', job_id, seed_draft_hash]`. This is not an ATX map row and never grants an Editor target. It stays stable throughout the job. The last actual heading preceding a byte supplies ownership for other units; canonical display anchors are not mutation or inventory identities.

Additional facts have exact spans and their own unit types: inline code identifiers, explicit section/document references, list-defined members, table rows/cells, preconditions, postconditions, acceptance labels, fenced schema fields, required members and enum members. Escaped-pipe parity distinguishes table delimiters from literal pipes. JSON token scanning rejects duplicate fields and malformed/trailing input rather than inventing a schema interpretation. It inventories fields and required/enum array members with exact UTF-8 spans, including Unicode and multiline values. YAML lexical discovery covers named/quoted field keys, block required/enum members (including indentless lists) and scalar flow members with quoted commas and comment tails. Unsupported nested/anchored flow members remain fully covered primary bytes and generate an explicit opaque-schema diagnostic. This is not a general YAML semantic interpreter.

Configured exact discovery rules have `rule_id`, single-line nonempty `text`, `normative` and `kind`. Kinds cover concepts, identifiers, commands, flags, fields, states, roles, error codes, terminal states, artifacts and normative patterns. Rule IDs are unique and rule array order is hash-bound. Discovery runs inside schema fences as well as prose. The consuming admission must bind the exact rule artifact before the Editor and carry its hash through inventory comparison; a free-form client instruction or an unbound optional runtime override cannot supply rules.

`protected-invariants-v2` carries the ordered `discovery_rules` array alongside the existing seed-bound assertion program. The descriptor's existing `protected_invariants_hash` binds the entire artifact, so rules participate transitively in D6 job identity without introducing an unbound configuration channel or changing the frozen job-ID formula. The assertion, inventory and candidate-assessment consumers derive rules from that artifact; an explicitly supplied rule list must match it exactly. `protected-invariants-v1` supplies an empty configured-rule list. Bootstrap may dispatch only a protected-invariants schema actually pinned by the selected validated suite; the frozen first suite does not include or accept v2.

Primary lines containing RFC requirement words (`MUST`, `SHALL`, `SHOULD`, `MAY`, `REQUIRED`, `RECOMMENDED`, `OPTIONAL`) or configured normative exact patterns are marked normative with their exact discovery source. Additional facts remain independently protected but do not add a second normative-budget count for the same primary statement. This classification is deterministic discovery, not proof of semantic force, equivalence or a natural-language concept. Semantic requirements without these markers require the separately bound annotation/assertion path promised by the candidate leaf; they must not be guessed into deterministic identity.

Seed unit IDs retain D6: seed hash, owning stable section ID, kind and same-kind ordinal. Canonical unit order sorts source start, primary before facts, source end and kind. Every unit points to the primary line on which it begins through `anchor_line_unit_id`; a multiline schema fact can extend beyond that anchor line. No inserted candidate unit may be reseeded using a changed draft hash. Candidate lineage must preserve unambiguous predecessor IDs and use transaction/operation/payload identities for additions.

`vnext-preservation-inventory-v2` binds job, seed/current hashes, section-map hash, frontmatter owner, discovery-rule hash, units and diagnostics. It also records `base_inventory_hash`, `transaction_id`, `proposal_hash` and sorted unique `retired_unit_ids`. The first three are all null for seed inventories and all populated for candidates; seed inventories have no retired IDs. Seed readback recomputes the entire packet, including IDs, classifications and anchor references. Candidate readback reproduces the immediate transition as described below; schema validity alone does not establish provenance.

## Deterministic assertion program

Compile the assertion program before Editor invocation. Check the protected-invariants schema, immutable seed hash, every D6 assertion ID, uniqueness, exact target scope and supported matcher grammar. An unsupported query is an invalid program; it must not be treated as a successful empty result.

`target_scope = document` selects all draft bytes. Other scopes select an exact seed canonical section anchor, optionally prefixed `section:`. The latter spelling disambiguates a heading whose anchor is literally `document`. Resolve the selected seed section to its stable ID, then find that same active ID in the candidate; a rename does not move an assertion to a different section. Missing/retired scope fails. Scoped counts cover the union of source and destination descendant IDs. These selectors introduce no wildcard or implicit source/authority alias.

The deterministic matcher grammar is closed:

- Heading presence/absence: `exact` compares heading text, `canonical_id` compares current display anchors, and `regex` matches individual heading texts.
- Identifier presence/absence: exact/canonical-ID/regex matching over discovered inline identifiers, schema fields/required members and configured lexical identifier kinds. A query is exactly `{kind, value}`, selecting a supported lexical fact kind and exact value. These assertions test tokens, not implicit semantic concepts.
- Enum-member presence: schema enum values and list-defined literal labels; a `{kind, value}` query may select another explicitly discovered fact kind.
- Table-row presence: an exact/regex row or an inventory query exactly `{cells: [...]}`. Cells retain order; Markdown punctuation escapes are decoded for literal cell comparison.
- Frozen section: `canonical_id` must equal the seed scope anchor, or `exact` must equal its exact seed-byte hash. Evaluation compares exact scoped candidate bytes against immutable seed bytes, not an intermediate changed base.
- Maximum changed sections/normative units: either an `exact` canonical nonnegative decimal string or an inventory query exactly `{maximum: integer}`. Booleans, leading zeroes and unknown fields are invalid. Missing change metrics fails the assertion. Document scope counts every change, including a non-addressable inventory owner.
- Canonical model count: a query exactly `{kind, value, count}` counts explicit matching lexical/structural facts. Its evidence identifies the count as syntactic; it cannot prove the absence of a hidden semantic model. Semantic model uniqueness remains a verifier assertion when structural facts are insufficient.

`concept_required` and `concept_forbidden` require semantic enforcement and a semantic matcher. Deterministic terminology checks must instead use explicit lexical identifier discovery/assertions. Semantic assertions produce no deterministic result and must receive exact independent-verifier coverage. Evaluation produces exactly one result for every deterministic assertion, including failures for parser diagnostics that make a scoped lexical query incomplete. It never promotes an unsupported or missing query to `not_applicable`.

## Mechanical inventory comparison (partial consumer)

The current comparison kernel recomputes every base discovery fact and scans candidate bytes independently. It uses the assembler's exact source pieces through the one final normalization to identify retained units by original source position. This preserves distinct repeated unchanged units without global text guessing. Inside one operation/owner/kind group, identical streams or a unique maximum exact-content alignment establish unchanged counterparts. A single changed unit between exact anchors has one structural predecessor. Other nonempty gaps require explicit correspondence; an ambiguous alignment fails rather than selecting a tie-breaker.

The comparison emits source unit IDs, temporary candidate scan ordinals, operation attribution, change classification and whether independent semantic evidence is required. Unresolved nonempty predecessor/successor gaps emit `ambiguous`, rather than claiming deletion merely because correspondence is missing. Independently known removals in other gaps remain visible and require authorization. It recognizes explicit RFC force reductions/increases between known levels. Removal of an RFC marker into unranked natural language remains a semantic obligation, not a deterministic claim of equivalence or weakening. Exact normalization-equivalent content is unchanged unless its owning section's ancestry moves. No output is a final preservation disposition or approval.

Retain baseline parser diagnostics separately when the seed itself contains an admitted syntax defect. A corrected candidate must have complete current discovery; the old diagnostic is evidence of incomplete baseline lexical interpretation for mandatory semantic review, not a permanent failure inherited by every repaired candidate. This does not certify that a syntax repair retained the seed's intended semantics. Candidate parser diagnostics remain failures.

Observed section changes come from changed unit owners. A changed normative primary unit counts once, including its source/destination section membership; newly added normative primary units count once. Overlapping lexical facts do not double-count the primary statement. Counts come from the recomputed units and correspondence, not Editor claims. The assertion evaluator can consume these real metrics. Until full correspondence is established, a failed comparison may conservatively expose unmatched old and new units; those diagnostic counts must not be presented as a passing candidate's final budget proof.

## Exact unit authorization

`vnext-mutation-decision-request-v2` adds the canonical base inventory hash and per-option `authorized_unit_effects` to the existing request bindings. Each grant names one exact inventory unit and one or both effects `remove` and `weaken`. Grant units must be inside an admitted target or its descendants; the frontmatter inventory owner cannot supply an Editor target. Duplicate, unknown and foreign units fail. Every global remove/weaken effect requires named units. The option's `authorized_deletion_unit_ids` is exactly the projection of grants containing `remove`; weakening grants do not imply removal permission.

The request ID uses the `whetstone-mutation-decision-v2` domain. `operator-decision-response-v1` remains unchanged: its exact request hash transitively binds the new inventory and named grants. Bind the chosen option, existing response identity, actor, rationale and immutable input hashes before deriving the editable finding. A response to a v1 request cannot be reinterpreted as a v2 named-unit grant. A deferral grants nothing and contributes no response to transaction identity.

Observed protected removals and known normative weakening require grants from the response-derived findings that the responsible operation actually addresses. The union of unrelated approvals in a plan cannot authorize an operation. Removal additionally requires the plan's exact deletion-unit projection. Revalidate request/response hashes, inventory and input bindings, derived finding identities, and the transaction's complete response set before checking effects. This is structural authorization validation; the caller must load authentic operator-owned artifacts and the semantic verifier must still check the exact final effect.

## Explicit correspondence and candidate unit lineage

The isolated v2 proposal path uses `mutation-plan-v2`, `patch-set-v2` and `patch_operation_v2`. The plan binds `base_inventory_hash` and `base_section_identity_map_hash` before D6 transaction identity is derived. Two bases with equal text but different lineage therefore cannot silently reuse transaction and inserted-unit identities. These versions are not added to the frozen first suite or advertised as public capabilities.

Every v2 operation has a `unit_correspondence` array, empty when unnecessary. Nonempty groups are allowed only in body/subtree replacements. Each group contains nonempty exact `base_unit_ids` and `payload_units` selectors `{kind, ordinal, content_hash}`. Ordinal is one-based in the complete raw payload inventory, including primary and overlapping lexical facts; it is not a same-kind ordinal or the ordinal after normalization. The Orchestrator rescans the exact payload using the same bound discovery rules. Every predecessor must lie inside its operation's replaced span, every selector must match its exact fact, and no endpoint may participate in two groups. Base IDs, selectors and groups receive canonical ordering before operation/proposal identities are computed.

The comparison resolves these endpoints through assembly origins and verifies that successor facts survive normalization. The group ID is `corr_` plus SHA-256 of canonical JSON `['whetstone-unit-correspondence-v2', transaction_id, operation_id, canonical_group_ordinal]`. Apply explicit groups before automatic matches. Same-kind one-to-one groups participate in ordinary change classification. Split, merge and kind-changing groups always create a semantic obligation; they do not certify equivalence, supersession, or authorization. An observed reduction from known RFC force levels remains a weakening obligation despite the correspondence claim. Count each changed normative primary predecessor once, rather than counting its successors again; if there are no normative predecessors, count normative primary additions.

Candidate inventory construction independently reassembles bytes, derives the section map, and reruns comparison. A comparison failure blocks a valid lineage inventory; it does not preclude retaining rejected candidate bytes and diagnostic evidence through the later registration/reporting consumer. Deltas must partition both complete inventories exactly. Same-kind one-to-one correspondence retains the predecessor unit ID, including provisional rewording and movement. These prospective identities do not become verified authority until the candidate is independently verified and promoted.

All other successors receive the D6 inserted-unit identity derived from transaction ID, creating operation ID, unit kind and full raw payload inventory ordinal. Each new unit must reproduce from that exact payload origin. Split/merge predecessors and all removed units become retired; prior retired IDs remain retired. A minted ID must differ from every active or retired base ID and every other minted ID. Rebuild all primary-line anchor references using the resulting IDs. Keep the original seed hash and frontmatter owner through successive candidates; never reseed changed text or resurrect a retired ID when old wording returns.

Candidate readback must exactly reproduce the packet, including base inventory/proposal bindings, IDs, tombstones and anchors. This validates one transition. The consuming registration/authority reader must validate the base's committed lineage back to the exact seed inventory; an individually well-shaped packet or successful immediate replay cannot establish that history. Independent semantic evidence must cover every explicit group and its exact base/successor units before a preservation disposition can pass.

## Deterministic candidate assessment and readback

The isolated assessor consumes immutable admission inputs plus the candidate bytes and section map read back by its caller. It rechecks the bound seed, authority, invariants, base inventory and map, plan and operator-response hashes, then independently reassembles the patch, derives section lineage and compares the exact result with candidate readback. A mismatched candidate, missing/altered map, changed payload or failed inventory comparison produces an explicit failed `preservation-report-v2`. Invalid admission/base inputs refuse before assessment; they cannot acquire authority through a candidate report. The storage consumer still must validate actual creation-event bindings, descriptor capabilities and committed base lineage. This component does not certify those unimplemented checks.

The v2 report retains the v1 minimum fields and adds exact base/candidate inventory hashes, protected-invariants and discovery-rule hashes, `metrics_complete`, outstanding semantic assertion IDs and baseline inventory diagnostics. Unit rows additionally record operation ID, successor IDs and candidate scan ordinals, explicit correspondence group ID and `semantic_required`. Every base unit has a row; newly minted units also have addition rows. A split/merge predecessor's successor arrays bind the complete structural group. These fields are obligations for the independent semantic consumer, not an Editor certification or a preservation disposition.

When lineage cannot be established, report `candidate_inventory_hash = null`; unidentified candidate additions have `unit_id = null` and exact scan ordinals. No passing identity is invented for a failed candidate. Unknown observed budgets are null and `metrics_complete = false`, rather than a misleading zero. Early reproduction failures retain ambiguous base-unit rows and all candidate/input hashes available at that boundary. A missing map has a null map hash. These nullable failure fields require the new schema; the frozen v1 schema is unchanged.

For a complete comparison, enforce the plan's actual operation, changed-section and distinct normative-primary-unit ceilings. New descendant sections count even when a single operation targets their parent. Revalidate operation attribution against the target's admitted section slice; other admitted findings cannot be borrowed from another slice. Check exact named-unit deletion/weakening grants and deterministic assertions. Blocking assertion failures fail the report; advisory results remain visible. An incomplete comparison cannot satisfy a count assertion. Changed unprotected terminal separators removed solely by the versioned normalization are trusted normalization effects, not operator-approved contract deletions.

`authorized` means the row passes deterministic surface/attribution/effect checks. It never means equivalent, verified, superseded, promoted or accepted. Semantic assertions are absent from deterministic assertion results and remain explicitly outstanding. Baseline syntax diagnostics remain evidence of incomplete baseline interpretation, requiring semantic coverage even when the repaired candidate's current inventory is complete. A deterministic pass with structural split/merge, rewording, movement or authorized weakening still requires independent semantic verification of those exact units and final effects.

Text hygiene reuses the existing forbidden-control/replacement-character validator. The current closed marker checks reject standalone bare/bracketed ellipses, abbreviated unchanged-section/blocked-editor markers, explicit unchanged-remainder statements, conflict markers and unapplied patch markers. They are deterministic rejection checks, not a semantic detector for arbitrary summaries or vague replacement prose. The independent verifier must still reject contract loss that lacks one of these literal forms.

Report readback recomputes both the report and candidate inventory from their exact inputs. Changing a verdict, suppressing a failure or semantic obligation, altering a tombstone or substituting candidate bytes cannot pass readback even if the artifact is well-shaped and rehashed. Reports and inventories remain unregistered evidence until the separate immutable storage consumer qualifies; their file presence cannot advance draft authority.

## Pre-Editor inventory admission and proposal transport

The isolated unit preflight consumes storage-admitted immutable inputs and the exact admitted base-pointer reference. It rechecks seed bytes/map, role configuration, source Reviewer/profile, invariant/rule binding and approved scope. Compile the assertion program and derive the complete seed inventory before any Editor call. For a later candidate base, require the base's committed inventory; changed bytes cannot be silently reseeded. Local fact validation supplements, but does not replace, the storage caller's committed-lineage verification.

Reclassify exact source feedback and advice. A finding requiring new immutable policy still requires a new job; unresolved operator choices still block editing. Bind named-unit responses to the newly derived inventory, the exact source finding and its actual feedback references before constructing the unit plan. Automatically include applicable normative authority and assertion-source context, then verify the selected context's byte hashes and roles. The prompt carries original finding details, derived-task provenance, exact operator requests/responses, the full inventory, rules and invariant program. Preamble inventory IDs remain non-addressable; inventory presence cannot expand an Editor target.

`vnext-proposal-response-v2` transports a `patch-set-client-v2` patch and exact inline payload text. Local operation labels and dependencies are canonicalized only after all bindings, shapes and correspondence selectors validate. Every referenced payload must appear once; unknown/duplicate paths and hashes fail. No replacement draft, inferred operator approval or Editor self-verification is accepted as authority.

The injected exercise uses a freshly claimed evidence directory and the existing non-registration publication helper. It writes the plan, full inventory and prompt before a second input read; invalid programs or changed pre-call inputs cause zero Editor calls. It retains exact raw response bytes before the post-call input read and response validation. A byte-level input fingerprint distinguishes byte values, JSON object/array structure and scalar values and includes the selected suite version/digest. Any changed admitted input, rule, context, plan or prompt prevents canonical publication. A native transport's configured role, executable, working directory, timeout and provider-schema hash are frozen too. Malformed responses and timeouts retain failure evidence. A previously claimed directory cannot restart the allowance or silently repeat a call.

The D7 bounded exercise permits two total invocations: the initial call and at most one automatic JSON/schema-shape repair. Each invocation has its own persisted marker, prompt, raw response when available, telemetry when available, and validation outcome/error. The second prompt contains the precise shape error and the unchanged original prompt. Supplied contradictory job, transaction, base, plan or Editor bindings are binding failures even when their spelling also violates a schema format. Duplicate/unreferenced payloads, path or preimage failures, invalid patch semantics, timeouts, client/provider errors and persistence failures receive no automatic repair. A `ValueError` thrown by the transport itself is not a shape error. The same inputs and transport are rechecked before and after every invocation.

Claimed directories cannot be executed again, including after interruption with an invocation marker but no response. This prevents allowance reset; it does not implement technical continuation. The storage/scheduler consumer must later reconcile retained completed output and interrupted attempts under its published continuation rules, rather than allocate a fresh request for an unchanged rejected transaction. The loader must reread storage-owned immutable inputs and authority, and the consuming adapter must enforce descriptor capabilities and D8 exclusion around registration. The exercise does not register a candidate, verify semantics, promote, or qualify a public mode; a local fresh-directory claim is not a substitute for D8.

## Native patch Editor adapter

The native adapter accepts only the pinned `codex` or `claude-code` Editor identity and model, with an explicit positive timeout and configured executable. It reuses the existing process runners: Codex receives read-only sandbox, disabled web search and ephemeral-session arguments; Claude receives no tools and no session persistence. This is a response-producing role, not the legacy whole-draft Editor acceptance path. The prompt includes the exact Editor identity to copy into the patch.

The self-contained provider schema is derived from `vnext-proposal-response-v2` by resolving local references and projecting structural types, object fields/required keys, enums and nullable alternatives. Canonical contract validation retains all value, uniqueness, path, hash and authorization constraints; a provider's structured-output hint cannot waive them. The checked-in provider schema must reproduce from its source contract and is copied into a temporary immutable input for each process invocation. Its exact byte hash is part of the transport binding.

Return exact Codex response bytes or the exact Claude structured-output token span/decoded result body, without normalizing or repairing them. Keep process telemetry on success, timeout and nonzero exit. A Claude error envelope is a provider/process failure even if the process exits zero. The orchestrator retains telemetry separately from authority evidence. Configured client versions remain distinct from observed executable versions; live provider compatibility and exact release/client qualification remain part of the separately authorized activation gate.

## Remaining consumer obligations

The proposed [local storage kernel](vnext_local_storage_kernel_v1.md) supplies isolated macOS/APFS exclusion and publication primitives with a permanent physical binding, fork-safe ownership, immutable replay and explicit uncertain outcomes. Its domain callbacks still require complete committed-lineage and transition validation. Neither the kernel nor its process/fault tests closes D8, registers a candidate or activates a consuming suite.

The isolated [candidate registration journal](vnext_candidate_registration_v1.md) connects persisted candidate/map/report/inventory readback to initial unverified disposition, contiguous creation events, exact replay and sole-orphan recovery under that kernel. Its fixtures exercise actual registration files under an unregistered test suite; full admission, committed base-history loading, D7 continuation, D8 audit and public proposal scheduling remain open.

The [durable Editor request journal](vnext_proposal_requests_v1.md) binds job/transaction-wide invocation slots across processes and resumes, supports explicit bounded technical continuation and exact completed-output reuse, and exposes read-only inspection. It connects to the native transport in scripted-process tests. Seed admission now supplies the full bound inputs; public configuration and continuation reporting remain consuming work. The journal cannot activate a mode by itself.

The [completed-request handoff](vnext_proposal_handoff_v1.md) now connects that journal to round plan/proposal artifacts and the candidate registry. It requires exact completed-request provenance, repeats source and handoff durability barriers, and commits an immutable round receipt before candidate registration. Its current-base loader now connects to [seed admission and staging](vnext_proposal_admission_v1.md) and [D7 closeout](vnext_proposal_closeout_v1.md). Historical promoted-base loading and public activation are not implied.

The connected private seed-proposal journey now includes preflight, native role transport, bounded retries, immutable request/handoff/registration, exact candidate/map/inventory readback, assembly-failure evidence, first decisions and terminal projections. The [guarded command interface](vnext_proposal_cli_v1.md) connects package admission, configuration, status and continuation; [explicit operator interaction](vnext_operator_decisions_v1.md) binds concrete requests and selected responses before derived-task admission. Before slice 18.5 can ship, qualify registration/recovery under the audited and reconciled D8 contract and publish the completed consuming suite and public proposal-only journey. Configured unmarked semantics must remain bound lexical discovery rules or semantic assertions; no unversioned annotation may invent identity. Independent semantic validation remains the 18.6 gate. No item in this supplement closes those gates by declaration.
